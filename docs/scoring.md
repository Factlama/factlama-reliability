# Scoring and aggregate verdict v0.1

Scores describe evaluated evidence, not absolute truth probabilities. v0.1 `groundedness` is the count of `SUPPORTED` applicable claims divided by the count of all applicable claims. `CONTRADICTED`, `UNSUPPORTED`, and `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` contribute zero. `NOT_APPLICABLE` is excluded. When the denominator is zero, groundedness is `NOT_APPLICABLE`, without a value. `hallucination_risk` is the fraction of applicable claims labeled `UNSUPPORTED` or `CONTRADICTED`; `contradiction_risk` is the fraction labeled `CONTRADICTED`. These are descriptive ratios, not calibrated likelihoods. Other dimensions are `UNAVAILABLE` until an evaluator and method version explicitly supply them. Round only at presentation; preserve full internal precision.

If any evaluation stage has a technical failure, the result is `ABSTAINED/ABSTAIN`, and all aggregate scores affected by the failure are `UNAVAILABLE`. Otherwise: no applicable claims or all claims `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` yields `ABSTAIN`; any `CONTRADICTED` yields `FAIL`; all applicable claims `SUPPORTED` yields `PASS`; any mixture with `UNSUPPORTED` or `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` yields `PARTIAL`. A request with no evidence and no independently checkable claims yields `ABSTAIN`. A policy may choose `BLOCK` on `PARTIAL`, but does not rewrite the factual verdict.

Every score includes `method_version`, input counts and optional uncertainty metadata in provenance. Changes to the formulas or verdict precedence require a version bump, golden-set comparison and an ADR if public semantics change. Benchmark calibration may later add probability-like scores under separate names; never relabel these v0.1 ratios as confidence.
